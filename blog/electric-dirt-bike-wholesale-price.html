Electric Dirt Bike Wholesale Price: What Three Real Models Cost Per Unit

Updated October 25, 2026·All notes

An electric dirt bike wholesale price is only a real number when three things are attached to it: the quantity tier, the model, and the trade term. Strip any one of those away and what is left is a marketing figure that disappears the moment you ask for a quotation.

This note publishes three prices we can actually stand behind, because they are attached to three production models made in our Wuyi partner factory — not to a catalogue rendering. All three are listed with their real quantity tiers and their real minimum order quantity, including the one model whose MOQ is not 1.

Why most wholesale price lists you will see are useless

A price list with a single number per model is usually one of two things: the price at a quantity you cannot reach, or a price for a specification you will not be allowed to buy. The number is not wrong. It is just missing the two conditions that decide whether you can have it.

The second common failure is quieter. A supplier quotes FOB, you budget it as landed cost, and the gap between the two — freight, destination charges, duty, local homologation — arrives as a surprise three weeks later. That is not a pricing problem, it is a definition problem, and it is worth resolving before you compare anyone's numbers.

If you only need the models themselves, the three priced models are listed together with the MOQ of each one next to its price band.

The three price structures you will actually meet

Flat price. One number at every quantity. Rare, and usually a statement about how the factory runs the line: there is no setup saving left to pass on to you.

Tiered price. The number drops as quantity rises, in published bands. This is the normal structure for assembled vehicles, because a larger run spreads line setup and material purchasing across more units.

Quote on request. No published number. Twenty-one of the twenty-four products we list fall here, and the reason is simple: the factory has not given us a price we are willing to publish. We would rather write nothing than publish a figure that turns out to be unattainable. For the three models below, the price is real and you can plan against it.

Model 1 — Mimbob EM22-6 72V: $2,199 down to $1,350

The full-size option: 72V 27Ah lithium battery, 3000W/5000W motor, 80 km/h top speed, 80–90 km range, 150 kg load capacity. Minimum order quantity is 1, so a single unit for testing is a legitimate order rather than a favour.

The tier structure is where the money is. At 1–9 units the price is $2,199. At 10–49 it drops to $1,550 — a $649 saving per unit, or $6,490 across a ten-unit order. At 50+ it reaches $1,350, a further $200 per unit below the middle band. If your plan is ten units anyway, the difference between ordering nine and ordering ten is larger than the cost of the tenth bike.

Model 2 — Nexvey Flash Pro 60V: $1,599 at every quantity

60V 27Ah lithium battery, 3000W/5000W motor, 55 km/h top speed, 120 kg load capacity, and custom logo supported according to the listing. Minimum order quantity 1. The price is $1,599 flat — no bands.

A flat price is worth reading carefully, because it tells you where this model sits in the factory's schedule. There is no quantity discount to negotiate, so the honest question is not "what is your best price at 50 units" but "what changes at 50 units" — and the answer is usually specification and packaging rather than unit price.

Model 3 — Mimbob TK5 48V: $1,090 to $1,010, and an 80-unit floor

The mini: 48V 22.5Ah lithium battery, 2000W mid-drive motor, 3-speed, 30 km/h top speed, 14-inch front and 12-inch rear wheels, disc brake. At 80–99 units the price is $1,090; at 100+ it is $1,010.

Two things make this model different from the other two. First, the minimum order quantity is 80, not 1 — so the entry ticket is a real commitment rather than a sample. Second, the gap between the two tiers is only $80 per unit, which means the tier is not where the margin is; the model's lower unit price is. A 30 km/h, 48V bike and an 80 km/h, 72V bike are not competing for the same buyer, and buyers normally choose between them by target market rather than by preference.

What moves your unit price after the first quote

Four things move the number, and only one of them is negotiation.

  • Crossing a quantity band. The only lever the published price already reflects. Nine units and ten units are different prices; fifty and forty-nine are different prices.
  • Battery and motor specification. A different battery capacity or motor rating is a different bill of materials. It is a new quotation, not a discount on the old one.
  • Logo, colour and decals. OEM is supported on all three models, and the Flash Pro states custom logo support in its listing. Each change is quoted, because each one is real work at the factory.
  • Packaging and assembly state. How the bike is crated affects both handling cost and how much work you do at the other end.

If a supplier cuts the price without changing any of those four, ask what was removed. Something usually was.

What the FOB number includes, and what it leaves out

All three prices above are FOB Ningbo. That covers the factory cost, the inland haul to the port and export clearance. It does not cover ocean freight, insurance, destination port charges, import duty or local homologation and registration in your market.

If you want one number at your door, ask for CIF or DDP and compare those, not the FOB figure. We quote all three terms and consolidate multiple factories into one Ningbo shipment, which is usually what makes a mixed order affordable. For the battery documentation that has to travel with an e-bike shipment, the factory issues it and we check it before the container is booked.

How the price gets checked before you pay

Price is checked against the physical lot, not against the brochure. Pre-shipment inspection runs at AQL 2.5 with a photo report, and it happens before the container is booked — if the lot fails, it does not ship.

Certification is a separate conversation from price. Units ship RoHS compliant according to the product listing, but the certificate itself belongs to the factory, not to us; we verify the document for your order and send you the copy before production starts. Payment terms follow the factory and the order, so they are written on your quotation rather than published here as a fixed percentage.

The commercial side is deliberately plain: buyers pay the factory price, and our revenue is a commission from the factory after the order is placed. A shortlist of three to five factories takes 48 hours, and the average sourcing cycle is seven days.

Questions buyers ask before a first order

What is the MOQ for an electric dirt bike?

It depends on the model. The Mimbob EM22-6 72V and the Nexvey Flash Pro 60V both start at 1 unit; the Mimbob TK5 48V starts at 80 units. MOQ is confirmed on the quotation for your specification.

Is the price per unit or per container?

Per unit, FOB Ningbo. The three published prices are factory FOB for the quantity tier shown. Sea freight, destination charges, duties and local homologation are not included.

Can I put my own logo and colours on the bike?

Yes, all three models are listed as OEM. The Flash Pro 60V states custom logo support in its listing. Colour, decals and packaging changes are confirmed per order, because each change can move the unit price.

Do the bikes come with CE, RoHS or DOT certificates?

Units ship RoHS compliant according to the product listing. The certificate itself belongs to the factory, not to us; we verify the document for your order and send you the copy before production.

How are the bikes inspected before shipment?

Pre-shipment inspection at AQL 2.5 with a photo report, done before the container is booked. If the lot fails, it does not ship.

Do you charge buyers a fee?

No. Buyers pay the factory price; our revenue is a commission from the factory after the order is placed.

Tell us your quantity and we will quote the tier you land in

Send the model, the quantity and your destination port. You get the price for that band, what it excludes, and the loading plan — before you commit to anything.
